What is blood donation?

Blood donation is the voluntary process of giving blood so that it can be used to help patients who need blood transfusions.

Donated blood can be used during emergencies, surgeries, medical treatments and for patients who require regular transfusions.

A single donation can help save lives and contribute to maintaining a stable blood supply.

Blood Types

Curious about blood types? - We are here to help!

A blood type is a classification of blood based on the presence or absence of specific inherited antigens on the surface of red blood cells. Blood groups are sorted by the ABO system (A, B, AB, O) and the Rh factor (positive or negative), creating eight main types. Type O- is the universal red blood cell donor, and type AB+ is the universal recipient.

Understanding Blood Types

Blood types are classified according to the ABO blood group system and the Rh factor. Together, they create eight main blood types.

ABO Blood Group System

The ABO system classifies blood into four main groups:

  • Type A
  • Type B
  • Type AB
  • Type O

The classification depends on specific antigens present on the surface of red blood cells.

Rh Factor

Blood is also classified according to the Rh factor.

  • Rh-positive (+)
  • Rh-negative (−)

Combining the ABO group with the Rh factor gives the eight main blood types.

Why do blood types matter?

Blood type compatibility is important when blood is given to another person. The recipient's blood type must be considered to help ensure a safe transfusion.

This is why hospitals need to know which blood groups are available and which blood groups are currently needed.
